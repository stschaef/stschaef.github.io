Theorem. Frobenius Reciprocity

category-theory adjunction families

Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 in a locally cartesian closed category, 𝐴 over 𝑋 and 𝑆 over 𝑌. The change of base adjoints satisfy

∑𝑓(𝐴×𝑓∗𝑆)≅(∑𝑓𝐴)×𝑆
∏𝑓(𝑓∗𝑆⇒𝐴)≅𝑆⇒∏𝑓𝐴

The first is the Frobenius property of the coproducts ∑𝑓 ([1], Lemma 1.9.12).

Proof. For the second, a map 𝑇→∏𝑓(𝑓∗𝑆⇒𝐴) corresponds to a map 𝑓∗𝑇×𝑓∗𝑆→𝐴, that is (as 𝑓∗ preserves products) to a map 𝑓∗(𝑇×𝑆)→𝐴, hence to 𝑇×𝑆→∏𝑓𝐴 and so to 𝑇→𝑆⇒∏𝑓𝐴. Both sides therefore represent the same presheaf, and are isomorphic by the Yoneda lemma (compare What is a universal property, really?). The first follows the same way: a map (∑𝑓𝐴)×𝑆→𝑇 corresponds to ∑𝑓𝐴→𝑆⇒𝑇, to 𝐴→𝑓∗(𝑆⇒𝑇), to 𝐴→𝑓∗𝑆⇒𝑓∗𝑇 (as 𝑓∗ preserves exponentials), to 𝐴×𝑓∗𝑆→𝑓∗𝑇 and so to ∑𝑓(𝐴×𝑓∗𝑆)→𝑇.

For families. In families of types both are direct calculations. At 𝑦:𝑌 the left side of the first is

∑𝑥:𝑋(𝑓𝑥=𝑦)×𝐴𝑥×𝑆(𝑓𝑥)

and transporting along 𝑓𝑥=𝑦 replaces 𝑆(𝑓𝑥) by 𝑆𝑦, which no longer depends on 𝑥 and leaves the sum. Similarly

∏𝑥:𝑋(𝑓𝑥=𝑦)→𝑆(𝑓𝑥)→𝐴𝑥≅𝑆𝑦→∏𝑥:𝑋(𝑓𝑥=𝑦)→𝐴𝑥.
