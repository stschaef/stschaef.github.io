The co-Eilenberg–Moore category as a displayed category co-eilenberg-moore-displayed

A comonad on 𝒞︀ is a monad 𝑊 on 𝒞︀op. Everything about its coalgebras is then inherited from the Eilenberg–Moore construction, instantiated at the opposite category — nothing is defined twice.

Algebras of 𝑊 over 𝒞︀op are coalgebras 𝛾∈𝒞︀(𝑥,𝑊𝑥) of the underlying endofunctor, and the monad algebra laws, read in 𝒞︀op, are the comonad coalgebra laws — the unit and multiplication of 𝑊, viewed in 𝒞︀, are the counit 𝜀 and comultiplication 𝛿:

𝛾⋆𝜀𝑥=𝗂𝖽𝑥𝛾⋆𝛿𝑥=𝛾⋆𝑊𝛾.

The co-Eilenberg–Moore category is the opposite of the total category:

coEM(𝑊)=(EM(𝑊))op.

Definition. Terminal coalgebra terminal-coalgebra

Fix an endofunctor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒞︀. A terminal 𝐹-coalgebra, written 𝜈𝐹, is a terminal object of the category of coalgebras Coalg(𝐹) — equivalently, an initial algebra for 𝐹op.

Unfolding the universal property: a terminal coalgebra is a coalgebra (𝜈𝐹,out) such that every coalgebra (𝑥,𝛾) admits a unique morphism unfold𝛾:𝑥→𝜈𝐹 satisfying

(unfold𝛾)⋆out=𝛾⋆𝐹(unfold𝛾).

Definition. Corecursive algebra corecursive-algebra

Fix an endofunctor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒞︀. An algebra 𝛼:𝐹𝐵→𝐵 is corecursive when for every coalgebra 𝛾:𝑋→𝐹𝑋 there is exactly one hylomorphism from 𝛾 to 𝛼. Equivalently, the functor 𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈(−,𝛼) of the hylomorphism profunctor is constantly a singleton. It is the dual of a recursive coalgebra: an 𝐹-algebra in 𝒞︀ is corecursive exactly when it is recursive as an 𝐹op-coalgebra in 𝒞︀op.

Example. If (𝜈𝐹,𝗈𝗎𝗍) is a terminal coalgebra, then 𝗈𝗎𝗍 is invertible and 𝗈𝗎𝗍−1:𝐹(𝜈𝐹)→𝜈𝐹 is a corecursive algebra: a solution of ℎ=𝛾⋆𝐹ℎ⋆𝗈𝗎𝗍−1 is the same thing as a solution of ℎ⋆𝗈𝗎𝗍=𝛾⋆𝐹ℎ, that is, a coalgebra map into the terminal coalgebra, and there is exactly one, 𝗎𝗇𝖿𝗈𝗅𝖽𝛾.

Definition. Hylomorphism hylomorphism

Fix an endofunctor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒞︀, a coalgebra 𝛾:𝑋→𝐹𝑋 and an algebra 𝛼:𝐹𝐵→𝐵. A hylomorphism (or coalgebra-to-algebra morphism) from 𝛾 to 𝛼 is a morphism ℎ:𝑋→𝐵 satisfying

ℎ=𝛾⋆𝐹ℎ⋆𝛼.

Definition. The hylomorphism profunctor hylomorphism-profunctor

Fix an endofunctor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒞︀. Hylomorphisms form a profunctor from coalgebras to algebras,

𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈:𝖢𝗈𝖺𝗅𝗀(𝐹)op×𝖠𝗅𝗀(𝐹)→𝐒𝐞𝐭,

where 𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈((𝑋,𝛾),(𝐵,𝛼)) is the set of ℎ:𝑋→𝐵 with ℎ=𝛾⋆𝐹ℎ⋆𝛼.

The action is by composition. If 𝑔:(𝑌,𝛿)→(𝑋,𝛾) is a coalgebra morphism (𝑔⋆𝛾=𝛿⋆𝐹𝑔) and 𝑘:(𝐵,𝛼)→(𝐵′,𝛼′) is an algebra morphism (𝛼⋆𝑘=𝐹𝑘⋆𝛼′), then 𝑔⋆ℎ⋆𝑘 is again a hylomorphism:

𝛿⋆𝐹(𝑔⋆ℎ⋆𝑘)⋆𝛼′=𝛿⋆𝐹𝑔⋆𝐹ℎ⋆𝛼⋆𝑘=𝑔⋆𝛾⋆𝐹ℎ⋆𝛼⋆𝑘=𝑔⋆ℎ⋆𝑘.

In these terms, a coalgebra 𝛾 is recursive when 𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈(𝛾,−) is the terminal functor, and an algebra 𝛼 is corecursive when 𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈(−,𝛼) is. For the inverse of an initial algebra the profunctor is representable, 𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈(𝗂𝗇−1,−)≅𝖠𝗅𝗀(𝐹)(𝗂𝗇,−), and dually 𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈(−,𝗈𝗎𝗍−1)≅𝖢𝗈𝖺𝗅𝗀(𝐹)(−,𝗈𝗎𝗍) for a terminal coalgebra.

When every value of 𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈 is a singleton, every divide-and-conquer specification over 𝐹 has exactly one solution. This is what local contractivity guarantees.

Definition. Recursive coalgebra recursive-coalgebra

Fix an endofunctor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒞︀. A coalgebra 𝛾:𝑋→𝐹𝑋 is recursive when for every algebra 𝛼:𝐹𝐵→𝐵 there is exactly one hylomorphism ℎ:𝑋→𝐵 from 𝛾 to 𝛼, that is, exactly one solution of

ℎ=𝛾⋆𝐹ℎ⋆𝛼.

Equivalently, the functor 𝖧𝗒𝗅𝗈(𝛾,−) of the hylomorphism profunctor is constantly a singleton.

Recursiveness is a coalgebraic form of well-foundedness: 𝛾 decomposes each input into subproblems, and recursiveness says that every divide-and-conquer program built on this decomposition has a unique meaning, without mentioning an order on inputs. [1] use recursive coalgebras on categories of indexed families to obtain algorithms that are correct by the type of the map they compute.

Example. If (𝜇𝐹,𝗂𝗇) is an initial algebra, then 𝗂𝗇 is invertible (Lambek’s lemma) and 𝗂𝗇−1:𝜇𝐹→𝐹(𝜇𝐹) is a recursive coalgebra. Precomposing with the isomorphism 𝗂𝗇, the equation ℎ=𝗂𝗇−1⋆𝐹ℎ⋆𝛼 is equivalent to 𝗂𝗇⋆ℎ=𝐹ℎ⋆𝛼, which says ℎ is an algebra map out of the initial algebra; there is exactly one, 𝖿𝗈𝗅𝖽𝛼.

The dual notion is a corecursive algebra.
