Definition. Dependent Tensor of Grammars

2026-10-01 · parsing lambekd intrinsically-correct

The tensor of grammars,

(𝐴⊗𝐵)(𝑤)=∑𝑤=𝑢𝑣𝐴(𝑢)×𝐵(𝑣)

is simply typed, so 𝐵 cannot see what 𝐴 matched.

Just as we have the generalization from the pair type 𝐴×𝐵 to the dependent pair Σ𝑎:𝐴𝐵𝑎, we can define a dependent tensor operation. It is a Σ-like generalization over the ordinary tensor, in which the second grammar depends on a parse tree of the first.

Write 𝖳𝗋𝖾𝖾(𝐴)=∑𝑤𝐴(𝑤) for the type of parse trees of 𝐴, each paired with the string it parses.

For a grammar 𝐴 and a family of grammars 𝐵:𝖳𝗋𝖾𝖾(𝐴)→𝐆𝐫, the dependent tensor is:

(𝐴⊗𝖣𝐵)(𝑤)=∑𝑤=𝑢𝑣∑𝑎:𝐴(𝑢)𝐵(𝑢,𝑎)(𝑣)

We may also write it as (𝑎∈𝐴)⊗𝐵(𝑎).

When 𝐵 is a constant family, the dependent tensor reduces to the ordinary tensor 𝐴⊗𝐵.

Dependence runs left to right here, which fits left-to-right parsing. The other handedness, in which the first grammar depends on a parse of the second, is also definable.

Relation to Day Convolution

Just as the ordinary tensor is given by Day convolution, I think there is a similar dependent Day convolution for which this operation is an instance. My best guess is for any 𝑋 presheaf on a monoidal category 𝒞︀ and 𝑌 a functor from the category of elements of 𝑋 into presheaves on 𝒞︀,

(𝑋⊗𝖣𝑌)𝑐=∫𝑢,𝑣𝒞︀[𝑐,𝑢⊗𝑣]×∫𝑥:𝑋𝑢𝑌(𝑢,𝑥)𝑣

I’m not positive on this though, and I would hope to also extend this to enrichments that aren’t 𝐒𝐞𝐭 but I don’t see how that could make sense given that I have quantified over the element 𝑥:𝑋𝑢 in the coend rather than using the tensor of the enriching category.
