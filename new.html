1 Predecessors simplify later predecessors-simplify-later

A predecessor of 𝑥 is a top element of its strict downset: a strict morphism 𝜌:𝑝→𝑥 through which every strict morphism into 𝑥 factors uniquely. Equivalently, 𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝖼𝗍𝖣𝗈𝗐𝗇(𝑥)≅𝗒𝑝 — the downset is representable.

The Yoneda lemma then collapses later to evaluation:

⊳𝑃(𝑥)=𝖯𝗌𝗁𝒞︀(𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝖼𝗍𝖣𝗈𝗐𝗇(𝑥),𝑃)≅𝖯𝗌𝗁𝒞︀(𝗒𝑝,𝑃)≅𝑃(𝑝),

with 𝗇𝖾𝗑𝗍 becoming restriction along 𝜌. The name is from the naturals: every strict map into 𝑛+1 factors through 𝑛→𝑛+1, so on 𝜔 — in the topos of trees — later is just the shift

⊳𝑃(0)≅⊤,⊳𝑃(𝑛+1)≅𝑃(𝑛),

and a Löb step is a base value together with a rule producing the value at 𝑛+1 from the value at 𝑛.

When this predecessor exists, we can give a simpler description of later, as in the topos of trees, but this may not be possible in all direct categories.

2 Algebras as a displayed category algebras-displayed

Fix an endofunctor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒞︀. The 𝐹-algebras form a displayed category AlgStr(𝐹) over 𝒞︀.

Over an object 𝑥, a displayed object of AlgStr(𝐹) is a structure map

𝛼∈𝒞︀(𝐹𝑥,𝑥).

Over 𝑓:𝑥→𝑦, a displayed morphism from 𝛼 to 𝛽 is the proposition that 𝑓 is an algebra homomorphism:

𝛼⋆𝑓=𝐹𝑓⋆𝛽.

The total category Alg(𝐹)=∫AlgStr(𝐹) is the category of 𝐹-algebras.

3 The co-Eilenberg–Moore category as a displayed category co-eilenberg-moore-displayed

A comonad on 𝒞︀ is a monad 𝑊 on 𝒞︀op. Everything about its coalgebras is then inherited from the Eilenberg–Moore construction, instantiated at the opposite category — nothing is defined twice.

Algebras of 𝑊 over 𝒞︀op are coalgebras 𝛾∈𝒞︀(𝑥,𝑊𝑥) of the underlying endofunctor, and the monad algebra laws, read in 𝒞︀op, are the comonad coalgebra laws — the unit and multiplication of 𝑊, viewed in 𝒞︀, are the counit 𝜀 and comultiplication 𝛿:

𝛾⋆𝜀𝑥=𝗂𝖽𝑥𝛾⋆𝛿𝑥=𝛾⋆𝑊𝛾.

The co-Eilenberg–Moore category is the opposite of the total category:

coEM(𝑊)=(EM(𝑊))op.

4 Coalgebras as a displayed category coalgebras-displayed

Fix an endofunctor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒞︀. Coalgebras require no new construction: a coalgebra is an algebra in the opposite category. Define

CoalgStr(𝐹)=AlgStr(𝐹op),

a displayed category over 𝒞︀op, where 𝐹op:𝒞︀op→𝒞︀op is 𝐹 acting on the opposite category.

Concretely, over an object 𝑥 a displayed object is a structure map

𝛾∈𝒞︀(𝑥,𝐹𝑥).

The category of coalgebras is the opposite of the total category:

Coalg(𝐹)=(∫CoalgStr(𝐹))op.

The outer opposite returns morphisms to the direction of 𝒞︀: a morphism (𝑥,𝛾)→(𝑦,𝛿) is a map 𝑓:𝑥→𝑦 with

𝑓⋆𝛿=𝛾⋆𝐹𝑓.

Definition 5. The comparison functor of an adjunction comparison-functor

An adjunction 𝐹⊣𝑈 with 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ and 𝑈:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ induces a monad 𝑇=𝑈∘𝐹 on 𝒞︀. Write 𝜀:𝐹∘𝑈⇒𝖨𝖽 for the counit of the adjunction. Every object 𝑑 of 𝒟︀ then induces a 𝑇-algebra carried by the object 𝑈𝑑, witnessed by the map

𝑈𝜀𝑑:𝑈𝐹𝑈𝑑→𝑈𝑑.

This assignment extends to a functor into the Eilenberg–Moore category,

𝐾:𝒟︀→EM(𝑇),

the comparison functor of the adjunction.

Dually, an adjunction induces a comonad on the other side and a comparison into the co-Eilenberg–Moore category. When these comparisons are equivalences we say that the adjunction 𝐹⊣𝑈 is (co)monadic.

Definition 6. Direct categories direct-category

A well-founded order is a set 𝐷 with a proposition-valued transitive relation < admitting no infinite descent: every element is accessible. Write 𝑎≤𝑏 for (𝑎<𝑏)∨(𝑎=𝑏).

A direct structure on a category 𝒞︀ over (𝐷,<) is a functor

deg:𝒞︀→(𝐷,≤)

into the well-founded order viewed as a poset category. The functor organizes two pieces of data at once: an ordering on the objects, and the invariant that morphisms respect it — 𝑓:𝑥→𝑦 forces deg𝑥≤deg𝑦.

A direct structure equips the objects with a well-founded strict relation

𝑥≺𝑦⟺deg𝑥<deg𝑦.

Intuitively, direct categories are the right generalization of well-foundedness to the categorical setting: a direct category is essentially one whose underlying graph is a directed acyclic graph, layered by degree, so that data at an object may be defined by recursion from data at all objects strictly below it.

The degrees order the objects, while the morphisms of 𝒞︀ say how an object sits over its predecessors.

8 The Eilenberg–Moore category as a displayed category eilenberg-moore-displayed

Fix a monad (𝑇,𝜂,𝜇) on 𝒞︀. Its Eilenberg–Moore category arises in two displayed layers. The first layer is the displayed category of algebras AlgStr(𝑇) of the underlying endofunctor.

The second layer, EMStr(𝑇), is displayed over the total category Alg(𝑇). Over an algebra (𝑥,𝛼) the displayed objects are the propositions that 𝛼 satisfies the monad algebra laws:

𝜂𝑥⋆𝛼=𝗂𝖽𝑥𝜇𝑥⋆𝛼=𝑇𝛼⋆𝛼.

The Eilenberg–Moore category is the total category of the tower:

EM(𝑇)=∫EMStr(𝑇).

Definition 9. Initial algebra initial-algebra

Fix an endofunctor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒞︀. An initial 𝐹-algebra, written 𝜇𝐹, is an initial object of the category of algebras Alg(𝐹).

Unfolding the universal property: an initial algebra is an algebra (𝜇𝐹,in) such that every algebra (𝑥,𝛼) admits a unique morphism fold𝛼:𝜇𝐹→𝑥 satisfying

in⋆(fold𝛼)=𝐹(fold𝛼)⋆𝛼.

Definition 11. Later on presheaves later-presheaf

The later modality on presheaves is given at each object by mapping out of the strict downset:

(⊳𝑃)(𝑥)=𝖯𝗌𝗁𝒞︀(𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝖼𝗍𝖣𝗈𝗐𝗇(𝑥),𝑃).

An element of ⊳𝑃 at 𝑥 is a coherent choice of 𝑃-elements for all objects strictly smaller than 𝑥.

Restriction in ⊳𝑃 along 𝑓:𝑦→𝑥 precomposes with the induced map 𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝖼𝗍𝖣𝗈𝗐𝗇(𝑦)→𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝖼𝗍𝖣𝗈𝗐𝗇(𝑥). At an object of minimal degree the strict downset is empty, so ⊳𝑃 is trivial there.

Via functoriality, every presheaf restricts to smaller indices: the map

𝗇𝖾𝗑𝗍:𝑃→⊳𝑃

sends an element 𝑝 over 𝑥 to the family of all its restrictions along morphisms from strictly lower objects.

Theorem 13. Löb induction for presheaves on a direct category lob-presheaf

Let 𝒞︀ be a direct category and 𝑃 a presheaf on it. Every map

𝜑:⊳𝑃→𝑃

has a fixed point: a global element 𝗅ö𝖻𝜑:⊤→𝑃 with

𝗅ö𝖻𝜑=𝗅ö𝖻𝜑⋆𝗇𝖾𝗑𝗍⋆𝜑,

The hypothesis says: the value of 𝑃 at any object is determined by its values over the strict past — 𝜑 turns a coherent family over the strict downset of 𝑥 into a value at 𝑥. Definition 15. Monadicity and comonadicity monadicity-comonadicity

An adjunction 𝐹⊣𝑈 with 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ and 𝑈:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ induces a monad 𝑇=𝑈∘𝐹 on 𝒞︀, and a comparison functor

𝐾:𝒟︀→EM(𝑇)

sending each object of 𝒟︀ to the 𝑇-algebra it carries.

The functor 𝑈 is monadic when 𝐾 is an equivalence: the adjunction exhibits 𝒟︀ as objects of 𝒞︀ equipped with algebraic structure for 𝑇, the Eilenberg–Moore category.

Comonadicity is monadicity in the opposite category: a left adjoint 𝐿:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ with right adjoint 𝑅 induces a comonad 𝑊=𝐿∘𝑅 on 𝒞︀, a comparison 𝒟︀→coEM(𝑊) into the co-Eilenberg–Moore category, and 𝐿 is comonadic when this comparison is an equivalence.

16 The adjoint triple between presheaves and families presheaf-family-adjoint-triple

A family over 𝒞︀ is a set 𝐴(𝑥) for each object 𝑥, with no action of morphisms. Families form a category Fam(𝒞︀): a morphism 𝐴→𝐵 is a function 𝐴(𝑥)→𝐵(𝑥) for each 𝑥.

Forgetting the restriction maps of a presheaf gives a functor

𝑈:𝖯𝗌𝗁𝒞︀→Fam(𝒞︀).

It has both a left and a right adjoint,

Free⊣𝑈⊣Cofree.

The two adjoints demonstrate different means of forcing a family to be functorial. The right adjoint universally quantifies over morphisms in,

Cofree(𝐴)(𝑥)=∏𝑦𝒞︀(𝑦,𝑥)→𝐴(𝑦),

with restriction along 𝑓 given by precomposition. The left adjoint instead existentially quantifiers over morphisms out:

Free(𝐴)(𝑥)=∑𝑦𝒞︀(𝑥,𝑦)×𝐴(𝑦),

with restriction acting on the first component. (For Free we ask that 𝒞︀ have a set of objects, so that this sum is a set and thus Free defines a presheaf.)

Theorem 17. Presheaves are monadic and comonadic over families presheaves-monadic-comonadic-over-families

The adjoint triple Free⊣𝑈⊣Cofree induces a monad 𝑇=𝑈∘Free and a comonad 𝑊=𝑈∘Cofree on Fam(𝒞︀).

Both comparison functors are equivalences: presheaves are the Eilenberg–Moore algebras of 𝑇 and the co-Eilenberg–Moore coalgebras of 𝑊,

𝖯𝗌𝗁𝒞︀≃EM(𝑇)𝖯𝗌𝗁𝒞︀≃coEM(𝑊).

So presheaves are both monadic and comonadic over families.

Reading the algebra structure concretely: a 𝑇-algebra on a family 𝐴 is a map ∑𝑦𝒞︀(𝑥,𝑦)×𝐴(𝑦)→𝐴(𝑥) for each 𝑥, subject to the monad algebra laws — that is, exactly a functorial action of restriction.

The comonadic reading is the same structure seen from the element’s side: a 𝑊-coalgebra is a map 𝐴(𝑥)→∏𝑦𝒞︀(𝑦,𝑥)→𝐴(𝑦), giving each value its restriction along every morphism into 𝑥. Where the monad says restriction acts on values, the comonad says a value already carries all of its restrictions — and the coalgebra laws say it does so coherently.

Definition 18. Proper and maximal sieves proper-maximal-sieve

The representable 𝗒𝑥 is itself a sieve on 𝑥. A sieve on 𝑥 is proper when it is not equal to the representable.

Say that a proper sieve is maximal when it contains all other proper sieves as a sub-sieve.

Definition 19. Sieves sieve

A sieve on an object 𝑥 of 𝒞︀ is a subobject of the representable presheaf 𝗒𝑥: a presheaf 𝑆 with a monic morphism 𝑆↣𝗒𝑥. Sieves are a generalization from the notion of ideal found in ring theory to category theory.

A morphism 𝑓:𝑦→𝑥 belongs to 𝑆, written 𝑆∋𝑓, when 𝑓 lies in the image of the inclusion at 𝑦. Because 𝑆 is a presheaf and the inclusion is natural, membership is closed under precomposition:

𝑆∋𝑓⇒𝑆∋𝑔⋆𝑓for every 𝑔:𝑧→𝑦.

A sieve is thus a “downward closed” collection of morphisms into 𝑥.

Sieves on 𝑥 are ordered by refinement: 𝑆⊆𝑇 when every morphism belonging to 𝑆 belongs to 𝑇.

If the direct structure is reflecting, then every proper sieve 𝑆 on 𝑥 refines into the strict downset:

For suppose 𝑆∋𝑓 with 𝑓:𝑦→𝑥 of equal degree. By reflection 𝑓 has a section 𝑠, and closure under precomposition gives 𝑆∋𝑠⋆𝑓=𝗂𝖽𝑥, contradicting properness. So every member of 𝑆 strictly raises degree, which is membership in 𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝖼𝗍𝖣𝗈𝗐𝗇(𝑥).
