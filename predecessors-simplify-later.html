Predecessors simplify later

2026-07-20 · Steven Schaefer · category-theory presheaf guarded-recursion well-founded

A predecessor of 𝑥 is a top element of its strict downset: a strict morphism 𝜌:𝑝→𝑥 through which every strict morphism into 𝑥 factors uniquely. Equivalently, 𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝖼𝗍𝖣𝗈𝗐𝗇(𝑥)≅𝗒𝑝 — the downset is representable.

The Yoneda lemma then collapses later to evaluation:

⊳𝑃(𝑥)=𝖯𝗌𝗁𝒞︀(𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝖼𝗍𝖣𝗈𝗐𝗇(𝑥),𝑃)≅𝖯𝗌𝗁𝒞︀(𝗒𝑝,𝑃)≅𝑃(𝑝),

with 𝗇𝖾𝗑𝗍 becoming restriction along 𝜌. The name is from the naturals: every strict map into 𝑛+1 factors through 𝑛→𝑛+1, so on 𝜔 — in the topos of trees — later is just the shift

⊳𝑃(0)≅⊤,⊳𝑃(𝑛+1)≅𝑃(𝑛),

and a Löb step is a base value together with a rule producing the value at 𝑛+1 from the value at 𝑛.

When this predecessor exists, we can give a simpler description of later, as in the topos of trees, but this may not be possible in all direct categories.
