Definition. The Quotient and Residual Coincidence in Day Convolution

2026-10-01 · category-theory

The Day quotient, 𝐷𝐴𝐵, takes 𝐴 to be a covariant functor and 𝐵 to be a contravariant one. While the residual 𝐴⊸𝐵 takes both to be contravariant.

This apparent variance mismatch disappears when we restrict to the groupoid core 𝐺=𝖼𝗈𝗋𝖾(𝐶), where variance is trivialized since 𝐺≅𝐺op.

For any functor 𝐹:𝐺→𝐒𝐞𝐭 on the core, we can freely extend it to both a presheaf and a covariant copresheaf on 𝐶 by left Kan extension along the respective inclusions 𝐺→𝐶op and 𝐺→𝐶:

𝗆𝗄𝖯𝗌𝗁(𝐹)(𝑐)=∫𝑠∈𝐺𝐶[𝑐,𝑠]×𝐹(𝑠)
𝗆𝗄𝖢𝗈𝗉𝗌𝗁(𝐹)(𝑐)=∫𝑠∈𝐺𝐶[𝑠,𝑐]×𝐹(𝑠)

By substituting these extensions into the definitions of the residual and the quotient, we obtain a general coincidence for any 𝐹:𝐺→𝐒𝐞𝐭 and 𝐵:𝐶op→𝐒𝐞𝐭:

𝗆𝗄𝖯𝗌𝗁(𝐹)⊸𝐵≅𝐷𝗆𝗄𝖢𝗈𝗉𝗌𝗁(𝐹)𝐵

This equivalence states that computing the residual against the presheaf extension of 𝐹 is perfectly isomorphic to taking the quotient by its covariant extension.

The Representable Case

Instantiating the above theorem at a representable functor on the core, 𝐹=𝐺[𝑥,−]: the co-Yoneda lemma says that the left Kan extensions compute to the representables on 𝐶:

𝗆𝗄𝖯𝗌𝗁(𝐺[𝑥,−])≅𝐶[−,𝑥]
𝗆𝗄𝖢𝗈𝗉𝗌𝗁(𝐺[𝑥,−])≅𝐶[𝑥,−]

Applying the general coincidence theorem, the left and right adjoints coincide precisely on (opposite-variance) representables:

(𝐶[−,𝑥]⊸𝐵)(𝑐)≅𝐵(𝑥⊗𝑐)≅(𝐷𝐶[𝑥,−]𝐵)(𝑐)

When 𝐶 is the discrete monoidal category of strings, this recovers the derivative of formal grammars.

In nominal sets, I suspect that this construction also describes name abstraction and the freshness quantifier, although I have not check all of the details of the proof.
